The End of the Paper Trail

Veteran Benefits Guide helps veterans file disability claims with the VA. The work ran on paper, and paper couldn't keep up with demand. I led the design of VBG Portal, the platform that brought the entire claim online, cutting processing time by nearly half and freeing veterans from leaning on staff at every turn.

DISCOVERY

Where the process broke down

The paper process only worked because staff carried every step by hand. As the first in-house designer, I interviewed every operations role that touches a claim; four frictions shaped everything that followed:

01documents

Days lost to paperwork

Requests for more documents after submission restarted the wait.

02forms

Disability Benefits Questionnaires too confusing to finish alone

Veterans leaned on case managers and revised them repeatedly.

03scheduling

No control over evaluations

Every scheduling change routed through a coordination team.

04status

Zero visibility into claim status

The only option was calling customer service.

Process 01: Straighten the path

Compress the journey to seven legible steps

Mapping the full journey exposed redundant steps, fragmented information, and heavy staff dependence. VBG Portal consolidates it into seven steps, with information collected once, up front.

Process 02: Build the spine

A dashboard framework that scales with the process

Seven steps needed a predictable structure. I tested two opposite frameworks, total transparency versus total focus, and shipped the combination.

Explored · set aside

The all-tasks checklist

A full checklist beside a detail pane: total transparency, but twenty tasks greet you on day one and progress reads as a percentage, not a place in a journey.

Final design · shipped

One step at a time

A thin stepper names every step and where you stand; below it, only the current step’s tasks, as cards with one clear action each. Status legible in one look.

Process 03: Retire the form

Turn the questionnaire into a conversation

Fourteen pages of clinical language became a guided conversation, rebuilt with our medical consultant as a small system of reusable question cards.

The starting point

Fourteen pages of paper

The audit came first: clinical terminology, no guidance anywhere, and one misread field meant weeks of delay.

Deconstruct

Map every field, translate every question

Every field mapped to its intent, every cluster became a plain-language question: fourteen pages regrouped into nine short sections.

Process 05: Book the evaluation

Scheduling that protects veterans and the business

Step 5 books C&P evaluations through the partner network: effortless for the veteran, every appointment kept inside the network the business runs on.

Chosen direction

Request, don't book: a three-state flow

Partner clinics confirm each appointment by hand, so instant booking would be a lie. The design tells the truth: you pick a real slot, the network confirms it, usually within a business day.

READY TO SCHEDULE

Chosen direction

Protecting the business: the area-only map

A visible clinic address would let a veteran book around VBG. The compromise: show enough to plan (area, travel time) but not enough to bypass the network.
