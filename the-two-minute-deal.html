The Two-Minute Deal

5 min readMVP shipped

A sponsorship between a nano-influencer and a local business took up to five weeks of DMs before any content existed. We built a mobile marketplace that closes the same deal in about two minutes — not by making negotiation faster, but by designing it out of the transaction.

CONTEXT

The marketplace nobody built for the small players

Influencer platforms are built for big brands and top creators. That leaves out the pair with the most natural fit: a creator with a few thousand engaged local followers, and the shop down the street who needs exactly those people. With no platform between them, both sides fell back on cold DMs, and a deal small enough to be casual took five weeks to close.

We set out to build the missing marketplace, with an MVP due in three months.

Problem · Where the time goes

Six weeks lost to negotiation and reporting

Interviews and surveys showed both sides bleeding time in the same place: negotiation ran up to five weeks, reporting another week; nobody could afford professional tools.

The journey map pointed at three phases worth attacking: negotiation, creation, measurement. The brief: streamline the collaboration itself.

Strategy

Assist the negotiation, run it, or delete it

“Fix the five weeks” had three plausible products inside it:

A Ruled out

A negotiation-assist tool

Templates, rate cards, in-app chat. Easiest to build — and it would have made the five weeks politer, not shorter.

The haggling survives.

B Ruled out

An agency-lite service

Humans matching both sides. It solves trust instantly and scales exactly as fast as we could hire — with three people, we become the five weeks.

Scales at the speed of hiring.

C Chosen

A self-serve marketplace with standardized deals

The only path where deal time does not scale with headcount — and the riskiest, because it bets everything on one assumption: both sides will accept terms nobody negotiated.

One bet, tested before we built.

SOLUTION · THE MECHANISM

Remove the negotiation, keep the trust

There was no existing product to fix: no listings, no contracts, no reporting. The whole deal had to be invented as a sequence: publish the terms so nobody negotiates, turn delivery into a checklist so nobody reports, and hold both in one place so nobody hunts.

How the idea became three screens

Each screen exists because one question had to be answered before the next could be asked, and each answer forced what the screen had to hold.

Solution · Why it works

Why both sides accept a price nobody negotiated

Fixed tiers work only if each side gains more from the standard than from haggling. Businesses get a menu for an unpriceable question — what is a post worth? — at a floor far below any agency quote. Creators get published terms and never pitch themselves again.

Each side trades a little pricing power for certainty — the one thing five weeks of DMs never produced. The tiers are not a price list; they are a peace treaty.

PHASE 02: IDEATE

Decide the mechanism before the screens

Ideation with the PM and engineering converged on the structural moves: tier-based contracts to kill negotiation, post-campaign Tasks to kill the report. Debating the mechanism, not the UI, made weak ideas cheap to throw away.

Reflection

Three months meant choosing imperfect answers on purpose, and saying so. Likes are not a real business metric: gameable, blind to sales. But a legible, launchable metric both sides understood beat a rigorous one we could not ship. Growth suggests the trade was right; verified redemptions and repeat-rate tracking were how we would pay the debt down. Early-stage maturity is not the perfect answer; it is knowing which imperfections you are shipping, and why.
