BACKGROUND

The marketplace nobody built for the small players

Existing platforms serve big brands and top creators; nano-influencers and small local businesses, the best-matched pair, were left to DMs and cold outreach. Our goal: bridge that gap with a mobile app, MVP in three months.

RESEARCH

Six weeks lost to negotiation and reporting

Interviews and surveys showed both sides bleeding time in the same place: negotiation ran up to five weeks, reporting another week; nobody could afford professional tools.

The journey map pointed at three phases worth attacking: negotiation, creation, measurement. The brief: streamline the collaboration itself.

The two low points became the design brief: remove negotiation, fold measurement into the deal.

SOLUTIONS

Remove the negotiation, keep the trust

There was no existing product to fix: no listings, no contracts, no reporting. The whole deal had to be invented as a sequence: publish the terms so nobody negotiates, turn delivery into a checklist so nobody reports, and hold both in one place so nobody hunts.

01

Tiers instead of negotiation

What is it worth?

Three published tiers (50+, 150+, 400+ likes), one tap to accept. The agent picks the reach they can afford; the creator never pitches a price.

02

A checklist instead of a report

How do I prove it?

Visit, create and post, screenshot the likes. Tasks done means deal closed; proof of work is a by-product of doing it, not a separate report.

03

One page instead of a scattered deal

Would I even go?

Business info, tiers, requirements and terms on one tabbed page: Details before commitment, Tasks after, nothing to hunt for in DMs.

How the idea became three screens

Each screen exists because one question had to be answered before the next could be asked, and each answer forced what the screen had to hold.

PHASE 02: IDEATE

Decide the mechanism before the screens

Ideation with the PM and engineering converged on the structural moves: tier-based contracts to kill negotiation, post-campaign Tasks to kill the report. Debating the mechanism, not the UI, made weak ideas cheap to throw away.

Reflection

Shipping an MVP in three months meant choosing imperfect answers on purpose, and saying so. “Likes” aren’t a real business metric, but a legible, launchable one beat a rigorous one we couldn’t ship. The growth numbers suggest the trade was right.
